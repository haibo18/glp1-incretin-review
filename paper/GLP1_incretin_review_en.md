## Abstract

Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) is an incretin hormone secreted by intestinal L-cells in response to nutrients. By activating the G-protein-coupled receptor GLP-1R, it stimulates insulin secretion **in a glucose-dependent manner**, suppresses glucagon release, slows gastric emptying, and centrally suppresses appetite. Native GLP-1 is rapidly degraded by dipeptidyl peptidase-4 (DPP-4) in circulation, with a half-life below 2 minutes. To overcome this limitation, researchers have developed degradation-resistant peptide analogues and multi-target agonists, transforming the pharmacotherapy of type 2 diabetes (T2DM) and obesity. This review systematically covers the molecular basis of the GLP-1 pathway, its discovery history, drug evolution, landmark clinical evidence, safety boundaries, and open questions, and assesses the case for an international science award.

**Keywords**: GLP-1; incretin; GLP-1 receptor agonist; type 2 diabetes; obesity; cardiovascular outcomes; semaglutide; tirzepatide

## 1. Introduction

Obesity and type 2 diabetes constitute the fastest-growing chronic metabolic burden worldwide. Historically, glucose-lowering drugs targeted hyperglycemia alone, while weight-loss drugs suffered from limited efficacy, poor tolerability, or unclear cardiovascular benefit. The core observation behind the incretin concept is that **oral glucose elicits far more insulin secretion than the same amount of intravenous glucose** — the "incretin effect" — demonstrating that the gut sends pro-insulin signals to the islets after meals.

Of the identified incretins, glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP-1 are the two primary mediators. Over the past four decades, GLP-1 has evolved from an obscure fragment cleaved from the proglucagon gene into a drug family covering glucose lowering, weight loss, and cardiovascular protection. Its translational chain traces the classic modern-medicine path: gene cloning → active-fragment identification → physiological mechanism → stabilized molecule design → pivotal clinical trials → indication expansion.

## 2. Molecular Basis and Physiological Mechanism

### 2.1 Precursor Processing and Secretion

GLP-1 is derived from proglucagon. Prohormone convertases cleave it differently across tissues; in intestinal L-cells and pancreatic α-cells it yields GLP-1, GLP-2, and related peptides. The major bioactive forms are **GLP-1(7-37)** and **GLP-1(7-36) amide**. L-cells line the small intestine and colon; upon stimulation by glucose, fatty acids, amino acids, and neuroendocrine signals, they release GLP-1 into the portal vein.

### 2.2 Receptor and Downstream Signaling

The GLP-1 receptor (GLP-1R) is a GPCR expressed mainly on pancreatic β-cells, but also on α-cells, the gastrointestinal tract, heart, vascular endothelium, and central nervous system. Ligand binding activates Gαs, raises intracellular cAMP, and through PKA and Epac regulates ion channels and vesicle release, ultimately driving glucose-dependent exocytosis of insulin granules.

### 2.3 Multiple Pharmacological Effects

GLP-1 acts through four parallel pathways:

1. **Glucose-dependent insulin secretion**: insulin release is enhanced only when glucose is elevated, weakening in hypoglycemia — the mechanistic basis for its low hypoglycemia risk;
2. **Glucagon suppression**: reducing hepatic glucose output;
3. **Delayed gastric emptying**: prolonging postprandial satiety and reducing eating rate and total intake;
4. **Central appetite suppression**: acting on hypothalamic and brainstem appetite centers to reduce hunger signals and increase satiety.

### 2.4 Metabolic Inactivation and Drug-Design Constraint

The N-terminal alanine at position 2 of native GLP-1 is the DPP-4 cleavage site. Once in circulation, it is rapidly degraded, with a **half-life shorter than 2 minutes**. This pharmacokinetic feature dictates that native peptide cannot be used therapeutically; long-acting forms require sequence modification, fatty-acid side-chain albumin binding, DPP-4-site replacement, or slow-release formulations.

## 4. Drug Evolution: From Native Peptide to Long-Acting Multi-Target Molecules

### 4.1 First Generation: exendin-4 and exenatide

exendin-4 is a 39-mer peptide isolated from Gila-monster saliva, with ~53% sequence homology to human GLP-1. Its N-terminal position 2 is glycine rather than alanine, making it resistant to DPP-4 cleavage and extending its duration. Exenatide, derived from it, became the first marketed GLP-1 receptor agonist in 2005, validating the target.

### 4.2 Humanized Long-Acting Analogues

- **Liraglutide**: highly homologous to human GLP-1; a C16 fatty-acid side chain attached via lysine enables albumin binding, extending half-life; once daily.
- **Dulaglutide**: Fc-fusion protein; once weekly.
- **Semaglutide**: optimized through fatty-acid side chains and amino-acid substitutions; half-life ~1 week; once-weekly subcutaneous dosing; also formulated for oral use with an absorption enhancer.
- High-dose semaglutide expanded the indication from glucose lowering to chronic weight management.

### 4.3 Multi-Target Agonists

Beyond single GLP-1 agonism, GIP/GLP-1 dual and GLP-1/GIP/glucagon triple agonists pursue stronger weight loss and metabolic improvement. Tirzepatide activates both GIPR and GLP-1R; triple agonists (e.g., retatrutide) reported higher weight-loss fractions in early trials, but long-term safety, lean-mass loss, and post-withdrawal rebound require late-stage confirmation.

## 5. Landmark Clinical Evidence

### 5.1 Glucose Lowering and Cardiovascular Outcomes

Cardiovascular outcome trials (CVOTs) evaluated major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE, typically a composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, and nonfatal stroke) in T2DM patients at high cardiovascular risk:

| Trial | Drug | Population | Main result |
|---|---|---|---|
| LEADER | Liraglutide | T2DM, high CV risk | MACE HR ≈ 0.87 (95% CI 0.78–0.97) |
| SUSTAIN-6 | Semaglutide weekly | T2DM, high CV risk | MACE HR ≈ 0.74, ~26% relative reduction (95% CI 0.58–0.95) |

Together, these support a cardiovascular benefit of GLP-1 drugs beyond glucose lowering, not fully explained by weight loss, suggesting parallel endothelial-protective, anti-inflammatory, and anti-atherosclerotic mechanisms.

### 5.2 Weight Loss

| Trial | Drug | Duration | Weight change (drug) | Placebo |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STEP 1 | Semaglutide 2.4 mg | 68 weeks | ~-14.9% | ~-2.4% |
| SURMOUNT-1 | Tirzepatide 5/10/15 mg | 72 weeks | -16.0% to -22.5% | ~-2.4% |

The magnitude represents an order-of-magnitude improvement over prior anti-obesity drugs, with significantly higher proportions of subjects achieving ≥5%, ≥10%, and ≥15% weight loss.

### 5.3 Exploring Indications

Evidence and exploratory studies point to HFpEF, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), chronic kidney disease, secondary prevention of atherosclerosis, and exploratory neurodegenerative-disease research. Most directions await large hard-endpoint trials.

## 6. Safety and Limitations

1. **GI effects**: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation are most common, dose-dependent, and tolerated with titration;
2. **Gallbladder events**: cholelithiasis and cholecystitis risk requires monitoring in weight-loss populations;
3. **Pancreatic safety**: pancreatitis signals remain debated in observational and meta-analytic data;
4. **Thyroid C-cell tumors**: C-cell hyperplasia and tumors observed in rodents; contraindication retained in human labeling;
5. **Lean-mass loss**: muscle mass declines alongside fat during rapid weight loss; long-term body-composition management and resistance training are insufficiently integrated;
6. **Post-withdrawal rebound**: appetite and weight partially recover after cessation, suggesting chronic metabolic management rather than a one-time cure;
7. **Access and equity**: price, injection burden, and long-term adherence are real constraints.

## 7. Open Questions and Outlook

- The relative central and peripheral weights of GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon signaling remain to be dissected;
- The optimal dosing strategy balancing weight loss, cardiovascular benefit, and body-composition preservation is individualized;
- Oral peptide absorption, non-peptide small-molecule GLP-1R allosteric modulators, and gene/cell therapies remain early;
- Long-term safety beyond 5–10 years, tumor risk, and post-withdrawal trajectories require cohort accumulation.

## 8. Case for a Science Award

By prize criteria, this direction simultaneously exhibits: **a clear basic-mechanism discovery** (identification of the active GLP-1 fragment, receptor and physiological function), **reproducible and quantitatively large clinical translation** (glucose lowering, weight loss, hard cardiovascular endpoints), **decades of time maturation** (1980s basic discovery → 2010s pivotal CVOT and weight-loss trials), and a clear candidate lineage (basic: Mojsov, Habener, Holst, Drucker; translational: Knudsen et al.). The 2024 Lasker Clinical Award already recognized the direction, an important leading indicator. Uncertainty remains: the committee may split the number and boundary between "basic physiological discovery" and "drug-development contribution," and the award year may slip with the pace of evidence maturation.
